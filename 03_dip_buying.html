Wait-for-the-Dip — Annual Cash/NIFTY 50 Switch

Sit in cash from 1 Jan; deploy 100% only if NIFTY 50 closes 10% below its year-start price; withdraw back to cash on 31 Dec. Repeats every year, compounding.

WHAT THIS SHOWS — plain-English recap of the rule being tested.

Every 1 January, the account starts the year entirely in cash (the prior year's ending balance carries forward — returns compound year to year). That year's reference price is NIFTY 50's close on the first trading day of the year. If, on any later day that year, the close falls to ▲ 10% below that reference price, 100% of the cash is deployed — filled at the next day's open. The position is then held until the year's last trading day, where it is ▲ withdrawn at that day's close ("31 Dec"), regardless of price. If the -10% level is never touched that year, the account simply sits in cash all year and earns 0% for it. Only one entry/exit cycle per year — a second dip after a rebound is not re-bought.

Read this before concluding "the strategy works"

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanical reasons behind the headline numbers, stated plainly.

The strategy only deployed cash in 7 of 18 years (39%), and even then only for part of each year — across the whole 2008-01-01–2026-08-21 span it held NIFTY for 29% of the days and sat in cash for the rest. Net return (+105.0% frictionless) is far below buy-and-hold (+294.7%) largely because of that — most of the time, this account earned nothing while the market moved. Its max drawdown is also far shallower (-54.0% vs. -59.9%), but that is mostly a side effect of rarely being invested, not evidence the -10% trigger is a skillful timing signal — a scattering of just 7 events (2 of them losses) is a very small sample to draw a conclusion from either way. Also note the profit is heavily concentrated: the top 5 triggered years account for 162% of total net profit.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the concrete ways this backtest can mislead you if taken at face value.

  • Only 7 triggered years exist in 18 years of history — any win rate, average win/loss, or top-year concentration figure above is computed on a very small sample and can shift sharply with one more or fewer event.
  • "Down 10% from the year-start close" and "one entry per year" were both judgment calls confirmed with you in chat — a peak-to-date trigger, an intraday-low trigger, or allowing re-entry after a rebound-then-redip would all change results, possibly a lot.
  • The entry fills at the next day's open (a reactive signal); the year-end exit is modelled at that day's own close (a scheduled, known-in-advance liquidation) — this is an intentional asymmetry, not an inconsistency, but it does mean the exit is not lagged the way the entry is.
  • NIFTY 50 is the raw index level (^NSEI), not directly tradable — a real position would need futures, options, or an ETF, each with its own costs and tracking error not modelled here.
  • The cost-loaded variant models only a flat 0.05% commission and a fixed one-tick slippage per fill — no bid-ask spread widening, no market impact, no India-specific transaction taxes (STT, stamp duty, GST on brokerage), no financing/borrow cost.
  • Idle cash (all the time the account is not deployed — the large majority of the period) is assumed to earn zero interest. A real cash balance earning even a modest risk-free rate would materially change net return and CAGR here.
  • Prices are unadjusted; no dividends are modelled for either the strategy or the buy-and-hold benchmark.
  • 2007 (partial year) is excluded entirely since NIFTY 50's available price history starts mid-September 2007, so there is no real "1 Jan" reference price for it. 2026-08-21's year (2026) is in progress and excluded from every statistic above — it is shown only in the year-by-year log, marked-to-market.
  • This is a single fixed rule tested once over history — there is no out-of-sample test and no check for whether "10%" or "one entry per year" happens to be curve-fit to this particular history.
