Smoothed Heikin-Ashi × the Hero — Does the Indicator Improve the Max Strategy?

Report 104's double-smoothed HA system bolted onto report 98's Max stack A (Midcap 150 Momentum 10, 200-EMA + gold, keep-if 6m > 0, exit rank 30). Sixteen combinations: HA as the regime, as an entry gate, as a review exit, and as a daily overlay. The answer is no — every version gives up return, and most give up drawdown too.

The damage scales with how much of the hero the indicator is allowed to touch. Why every combination loses

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the failure is structural, not a tuning problem. Four mechanisms explain the whole table.

  1. Frequency mismatch. The hero decides twice a year on 6- and 12-month momentum. A smoothed 10-day HA candle flips in days. At a June or December review, the HA colour is close to noise relative to the signal that picked the stock; gating a 6-month pick on a 10-day state removes strong names without adding information. The best HA variant still gives up 1.51pp.
  2. The ATR contraction filter is the most destructive. Momentum leaders are volatile by construction. Requiring ATR(14)/ATR(50) < 0.8 selects the quiet names inside the momentum top 10: CAGR collapses to +11.6% and the drawdown deepens to -31.1%. Report 104 found ATR useful as an entry-timing filter for a broad HA stock system; it is not a stock-selection filter for a concentrated momentum portfolio.
  3. A faster regime is not a better regime. Index HA raises regime exits from 65 to 104 and cuts CAGR by 11.05pp; the strict version (green plus close above the HA open) is worse still — 157 exits and +26.7% CAGR. The 200-EMA's slowness is a feature: it ignores bear-market rallies instead of trading them.
  4. Daily HA exits are a cost machine. The early-exit overlay makes 4,950 trades versus 705; the half-de-risk version makes 5,033. At 0.1% per side the friction alone compounds to a double-digit drag, and the timing benefit never covers it. The re-entry gate is the mildest overlay tested, and it still costs 2.98pp with a deeper drawdown.
▲ this is a statement about the combination, not about the indicator

HA is not a bad indicator. Report 104 shows the standalone system on the same Midcap 150 cache beating an equal-weight basket over 2016–2026: 27.8% CAGR versus 20.6%, with a 32.6% drawdown versus 40.8%, Sharpe 1.57 versus 1.19. That edge is real but 2020-dependent (ex-2020 it lags the basket), and it comes from selecting and timing individual stocks out of a broad universe — a different job from overlaying a concentrated 10-stock, twice-a-year momentum book.

The hero is also not fragile. It survives every overlay tested; the worst still compounds at +11.6%. The point is that the untouched hero is better, not that the combination blows up.

Caveats: Midcap 150 is today's constituent list applied backwards, so all absolute CAGRs are survivorship-inflated; this lab does not model tax; and the window is 2009–2026. Those caveats apply to every row, so the ranking is robust even though the absolute numbers are optimistic.

Scope. This lab tests the HA signal as a market regime, a stock gate at the review, and a daily index overlay. It does not bolt the standalone system's per-stock 12% stop and next-open red-flip exit onto the hero: the engine's daily sell rule parks proceeds until the next review, so that test would be dominated by a mechanical cash drag. The review-level HA keep filter is the closest fair test, and it already gives up 1.51pp. A per-stock stop is a separate risk rule, not the HA signal, and deserves its own lab.

Method and reproduction

Hero: report 98 Max stack A — midcap150, top 10, Jun/Dec reviews, keep-if 6m > 0, exit rank 30, NIFTY 50 200-EMA regime with 100% GOLDBEES park, 0.1% cost per side.

HA: report 104's smoothed Heikin-Ashi — EMA(10) applied twice to O/H/L/C; green when HA close > HA open; strict adds price > HA open; ATR contraction is ATR(14)/ATR(50) < 0.8. Index HA uses the same definition on NIFTY 50.

Every variant runs through the same ms_builder.py engine used by reports 93–98; the hero regression gate is re-run after the HA additions and still reproduces report 93 exactly. Window 2009-01-02–2026-10-16.

● keep them separate — the hero stays the hero, HA stays report 104

If you want HA exposure, run it as a separate sleeve with its own capital, not as a filter on the momentum book. The two systems have different horizons, different turnover and different jobs; combining them at the same decision point subtracts from both.

For the hero, report 98's Max stack A remains the best configuration in this repo. For HA, report 104 is the implementation to use. The correct combination is a portfolio of the two strategies, not an overlay of one on the other.
