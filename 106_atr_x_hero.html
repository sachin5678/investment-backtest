Why every ATR version loses

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the failure is structural. ATR contraction is a short-horizon timing idea; the hero is a twice-a-year cross-sectional momentum book.

  1. The gate is rare where the edge lives. Only 10.9% of the top-10 momentum names pass ATR<0.8 at a review, versus 18.1% of all ranked names. The engine fills the slot from lower ranks, so the average holding rank jumps from 6.3 to 39.1. You are no longer running the hero; you are running a lower-momentum, lower-volatility book.
  2. Cash drag compounds the substitution. ATR<0.8 averages 8.3 of 10 slots invested and 16.9% cash at review events. At ATR<0.5 the book is 98.4% cash and makes only 14 trades. The filter can leave the strategy effectively uninvested.
  3. Loosening the gate only removes the damage. ATR<0.9 costs 9.53pp, ATR<1.0 costs 9.34pp, ATR<1.1 costs 2.01pp. The keep filter at <1.2 is the least-bad at 1.12pp, with the same drawdown and a lower Sharpe. No threshold flips the sign.
  4. The opposite gate buys drawdown relief at a steep price. Buying ATR>1.0 cuts the max drawdown from -24.0% to -22.0%, but gives up 8.97pp of CAGR; Calmar falls from 1.67 to 1.41. If the goal is lower drawdown, the existing 200-EMA + gold does it more cheaply.
  5. Horizon mismatch, again. ATR(14)/ATR(50) tells you whether a stock has been quiet for a few weeks. The hero's ranking is built on 6- and 12-month momentum. The filter can replace the ranking, but it cannot improve it — and replacing it is what the table shows.
▲ this is a statement about ATR as a hero overlay, not about ATR as a standalone tool

Report 104 found ATR contraction useful inside the standalone HA stock system. That result is not contradicted here: the filter is being asked to do a different job. In a broad, 8-slot HA system, ATR contraction times individual entries out of a large opportunity set. In a concentrated 10-stock, twice-a-year momentum book, it removes the strongest names from a small set and replaces them with weaker ones. The instrument is the same; the portfolio construction is not.

Caveats: Midcap 150 is today's constituent list applied backwards, so absolute CAGRs are survivorship-inflated; this lab does not model tax; the window is 2009–2026. Those caveats apply to every row, so the ranking is robust even though the absolute numbers are optimistic.

Scope. This lab tests stock-level ATR(14)/ATR(50) as an entry gate and as a review keep rule, with thresholds from 0.5 to 1.2. It does not test an index-level ATR regime, ATR-based position sizing, or an ATR/12% stop-loss. Those are separate risk-design questions; the stop would also interact with the engine's review calendar and needs its own cash/refill treatment.

Method and reproduction

Hero: report 98 Max stack A — midcap150, top 10, Jun/Dec reviews, keep-if 6m > 0, exit rank 30, NIFTY 50 200-EMA regime with 100% GOLDBEES park, 0.1% cost per side.

ATR: ATR(14)/ATR(50) on each stock's high/low/close, simple rolling means — the same definition used in report 104. A value below 1.0 means recent volatility is below its 50-day norm (contraction); above 1.0 means expansion. The buy rule gates new entries at the review; the keep rule forces a review exit when the ratio is above/below the threshold.

Every variant runs through the same ms_builder.py engine used by reports 93–98. The hero regression gate is unchanged and still reproduces report 93 exactly. Window 2009-01-02–2026-10-16.
