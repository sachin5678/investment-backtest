How it is built

Strategy. Universe: today's NIFTY 100 constituents. Score: report 45's risk-adjusted 6-month and 12-month momentum, z-scored and averaged. Hold the top 5, 10 or 20 names equal-weight and rebalance once a year, in the last trading day of the chosen month. There are 12 possible calendars; December and June are charted in full and all 12 are tabulated.

Gold regime switch. When NIFTY 50 closes below its 200-day EMA the whole book moves to GOLDBEES until it closes back above, at which point a fresh top-N is bought. Same rule as reports 42–48, confirm days = 1. Without the switch the portfolio stays in stocks all year.

Execution. The headline numbers follow the project's next-open rule: a signal formed at day T's close is filled at T+1's open, old holdings sold and new ones bought together, with the overnight gap earned by the old position. The legacy same-close convention (reports 42–99) is shown alongside, untick it to hide it. The gap between the two is the price of not being able to trade at the very close that produced the signal.

Disclosures & limitations

The universe is today's NIFTY 100 applied backwards, so every absolute CAGR is survivorship-inflated — compare rows with each other, not with an index fund. No transaction costs, slippage or taxes are modelled (a regime flip sells and rebuys the whole book, so the switch's real-world cost is higher than a once-a-year rebalance's). The window starts in 2009-09 because the 252-day momentum lookback and all 12 calendars must have valid data.

Opens come from the same Yahoo download as the dividend-adjusted closes. A handful of bad prints (missing, zero, or more than 30–40% away from that day's close) were replaced with that day's close. Results depend heavily on the rebalance month — a single calendar is an anecdote; use the medians.

Top 10 with the switch is the best-behaved combination here, but it does not beat the unswitched version in every month: the switch is insurance against deep drawdowns, not a return engine. This is a proof-of-concept analysis, not investment advice.
