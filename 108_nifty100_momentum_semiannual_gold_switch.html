How it is built

Strategy. The same engine as report 107, on a twice-a-year cadence. Universe: today's NIFTY 100 constituents. Score: report 45's risk-adjusted 6-month and 12-month momentum, z-scored and averaged. Hold the top 10 or top 20 names equal-weight and rebalance in the last trading day of two months six apart. There are six possible calendars (Jan/Jul, Feb/Aug, Mar/Sep, Apr/Oct, May/Nov, Jun/Dec); Jun/Dec and Jan/Jul are charted in full and all six are tabulated. Top 5 is not repeated here; report 107 covers it on the yearly cadence.

Gold regime switch. When NIFTY 50 closes below its 200-day EMA the whole book moves to GOLDBEES until it closes back above, at which point a fresh top-N is bought. Same rule as reports 42–48, confirm days = 1. Without the switch the portfolio stays in stocks between rebalances.

Execution. The headline numbers follow the project's next-open rule: a signal formed at day T's close is filled at T+1's open, old holdings sold and new ones bought together, with the overnight gap earned by the old position. The legacy same-close convention (reports 42–99) is shown alongside — untick it to hide it. The gap between the two is the price of not being able to trade at the very close that produced the signal.

Disclosures & limitations

The universe is today's NIFTY 100 applied backwards, so every absolute CAGR is survivorship-inflated — compare rows with each other, not with an index fund. No transaction costs, slippage or taxes are modelled; a twice-a-year rebalance trades twice as much as report 107's yearly one, and a regime flip sells and rebuys the whole book, so real-world costs are higher than shown. The window starts in 2009-03 (six calendars, so an earlier first valid rebalance than the yearly report's 2009-09), which includes the 2009 recovery — so these CAGRs are not comparable one-for-one with report 107's.

Opens come from the same Yahoo download as the dividend-adjusted closes. A handful of bad prints (missing, zero, or more than 30–40% away from that day's close) were replaced with that day's close. Results still depend on the calendar — use the medians rather than any single row.

The switch is insurance against deep drawdowns, not a return engine: it helps top 10 on CAGR as well, but it costs return for top 20 in every calendar tested. This is a proof-of-concept analysis, not investment advice.
