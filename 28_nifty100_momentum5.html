The concentration question, and the honest result up front

● Momentum-5 beats Momentum-10 by 2.4pp CAGR ● but with a 3.8pp deeper drawdown and longer underwater stretch

Halving the portfolio from 10 stocks to 5, with no other change to the selection formula or rebalance schedule, is a direct test of whether concentrating a momentum bet into fewer, higher-conviction names pays off. Over 2008-12-31–2026-08-21, Momentum-5 compounded at +33.1% versus Momentum-10's +30.7% — a real, meaningful edge from concentration.

That edge is not free: Momentum-5's max drawdown (-39.1%) is deeper than Momentum-10's (-35.3%), and it spent longer underwater (909 days vs. 693 days) — exactly what basic diversification math predicts: fewer positions means each one's idiosyncratic move (good or bad) has twice the impact on the whole portfolio.

Why concentration cuts both ways

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Both portfolios use the identical formula to rank the same 100 stocks — Momentum-5 simply keeps only the top half of what Momentum-10 would have held. On average, whatever made those top 5 rank above positions 6-10 continued to be true often enough over 18 years for the extra concentration to pay off (2.4% points of extra CAGR). But that same logic means Momentum-5 has no cushion at all if even ONE of its 5 picks turns out to be a momentum trap (a stock whose recent strength was about to reverse) — a single bad pick is 20% of the book, versus 10% in the 10-stock version.

That's exactly the trade-off visible in the numbers: better average return, worse worst-case, and a longer stretch spent recovering from that worst case (909 days vs. 693 days). Neither number is "wrong" — this is the standard concentration-vs-diversification trade-off showing up in a real backtest, not a flaw in either construction.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Today's fixed NIFTY 100 constituent list is applied retroactively across the whole window (survivorship bias) — same disclosed approximation as report 12 and every other reconstruction in this project.
  • Equal weighting (not free-float market-cap x momentum score), same as report 12 — for a 5-stock book this makes each position's weight (20%) even more consequential than in the 10-stock version (10%).
  • No F&O-eligibility screen and June/December rebalance dates approximated as the last trading day of the month, same as report 12.
  • Zero transaction costs, slippage, or taxes modeled — a 5-stock book turning over completely at each rebalance is a more concentrated trading cost exposure per rupee invested than the 10-stock version, though the NUMBER of rebalances (36) is identical for both.
  • Prices are unadjusted; no dividends are modeled for any holding.
  • This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path — a different window could easily flip which of Momentum-5 or Momentum-10 comes out ahead; concentration amplifies whatever the underlying momentum signal did over THIS specific history, for better or worse.
