The strategy itself is unchanged — same top-10 selection by 6m/12m risk-adjusted momentum, same equal weighting, same June/December rebalance. What changes: at every rebalance, each position is funded 50% by your own capital and 50% borrowed via Kotak Neo's MTF at +9.7% p.a. — buying 2x the notional exposure per rupee of your own capital. Every real charge Kotak Neo and Indian exchanges actually levy is modeled: brokerage, STT, exchange transaction charges, SEBI fees, stamp duty, GST, and the MTF interest itself — all sourced and cited below.

The honest result, at the headline 30%-maintenance-margin assumption: CAGR rises from the frictionless +40.6% to +51.9% — well short of a clean 2x — while max drawdown deepens from -35.1% to -59.4%. 52 of 360 positions (14.4%) got forcibly liquidated by a margin call before ever reaching their scheduled rebalance, averaging a -59.9% loss on capital each time. See the honesty note below for why the maintenance-margin assumption itself changes this picture substantially — and why a LOOSER threshold isn't automatically safer.

The 30%-maintenance scenario generated 360 positions over 18 years — roughly 720 pledge/unpledge events (360 entries + 360 exits). At ₹23.60 per event, that's ₹16,992 in total flat fees over the whole 18-year run — on a ₹1,000,000 starting account, that's only about 1.70% of the STARTING capital, cumulative, not annualised. This flat fee matters far less than a percentage rate here — but it scales the OPPOSITE way from the % charges: a smaller real account pays the exact same ₹20+GST per event, so it's a proportionally bigger drag for a smaller account than for a larger one.

Why 2x leverage doesn't give you 2x the return — and why looser margin isn't automatically safer

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Ignoring costs and margin calls entirely, doubling each position's exposure would compound to roughly double the frictionless strategy's already-large multiple — arithmetically much more than "2x the CAGR" once compounded over 36 rebalances, since leverage amplifies BOTH the size of each period's gain and the base it compounds from. Real MTF eats into that in three separate ways: +0.24% + +0.22% of round-trip transaction costs on DOUBLE the notional (vs. cash), +9.69% p.a. interest on the borrowed half (averaging 4.37% of a position's own capital per ~6-month hold), and — the big one — margin calls that force-crystallize a loss at the worst possible moment instead of letting a position ride out a temporary dip.

That third effect is why the maintenance-margin ASSUMPTION matters so much, and why the relationship isn't what you'd first guess. At a 40% maintenance threshold (margin calls fire on roughly a 23.1%-37.5% range of price drops depending on the exact threshold — see the table above), 133 of 360 positions (36.9%) got force-sold — MORE than three times as often as at 20% maintenance (21 calls, 5.8%). A tighter (lower) maintenance requirement gives a volatile momentum stock more room to have an ordinary bad month WITHOUT being forced out — which is exactly why the 20% scenario ends up with the HIGHEST CAGR of the three (+59.5%) despite being the "loosest" margin rule, and the 40% scenario the lowest (+37.5%) despite being the "safest"-sounding one.

None of the three scenarios avoid a deep drawdown, though — all three land between -62.3% and -59.4%, meaningfully worse than the frictionless strategy's own -35.1%. The margin-call mechanism changes WHEN and HOW OFTEN losses get crystallized, but 2x leverage on an already-volatile momentum strategy is a deeper hole to climb out of under every assumption tested here.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • The maintenance-margin percentage is this report's one real assumption, not a published Kotak number. SEBI requires dynamic, per-stock VaR-based margins that move with each stock's own volatility — Kotak doesn't publish that full day-by-day schedule. 20%/30%/40% are plausible flat stand-ins tested as a sensitivity range, not the real rule.
  • No grace period or top-up behaviour is modeled — a real investor typically gets 1-2 trading days to add funds/collateral before forced liquidation; this backtest force-sells the instant the threshold breaks, the same no-discretion convention used for every mechanical rule in this project.
  • All 10 positions are assumed individually margin-monitored at the SAME maintenance percentage; in reality Kotak's margin requirement varies stock-by-stock based on each name's own VaR, so some of this project's picks would have tighter or looser real thresholds than the flat assumption used here.
  • Today's fixed NIFTY Midcap 150 constituent list is applied retroactively across the whole window (survivorship bias) — same disclosed approximation as every other reconstruction here.
  • The 1500+ MTF-eligible stock list changes over time and isn't applied retroactively here — this assumes every one of the strategy's picks would have been MTF-eligible throughout the full 2008-2026 window, which may not hold for every stock at every point in its history.
  • Equal weighting (not free-float market-cap x momentum score) and no F&O-eligibility screen, same as every other reconstruction in this project.
  • Prices are unadjusted; no dividends are modeled for any holding.
  • This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path — a different window, especially one with fewer sharp intra-period crashes, could make 2x leverage look considerably better relative to the frictionless strategy than it does here.
