● 53.6% average pick overlap with the original ▲ a genuine risk/return trade-off, not a straight downgrade

Unlike most other variants tested in this comparison series, front-loading recent momentum doesn't just move CAGR and drawdown in the same direction — it trades one for the other. CAGR comes in at +38.0% versus the original's +40.6% (2.6% points lower), but max drawdown is actually SHALLOWER at -33.9% versus -35.1% (1.1% points better).

The mechanism is intuitive: weighting the most recent 3 months at 50% makes the score react faster to a deteriorating trend (cutting exposure to a stock that's started rolling over sooner than a 12-month-anchored score would), which cushions the worst drawdowns a little — but it also means the score can chase a short-lived 3-month spike that later fades, giving up some of the longer-horizon trend-persistence edge the equal-weighted 6m/12m blend was capturing. Whether that trade-off is worth it depends on how much you value a shallower drawdown against a lower total return — this report doesn't declare a winner, just shows the trade honestly.

Why this one is a genuine trade-off, not a clean win or loss

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Every other variant in this comparison series (reports 33, 34, 36) moved CAGR and max drawdown in the SAME direction — better or worse on both, or a near-tie on both. This one splits: 2.6% points lower CAGR, but 1.1% points shallower drawdown. That's exactly what you'd expect from making the score react faster to recent price action: a 3-month-heavy score notices a stock rolling over sooner than a formula anchored more to the past 12 months, so it drops that stock from the top 10 a bit earlier in a real decline — cushioning the worst drawdowns — but it also means the score gives real weight to short 3-month moves that sometimes reverse before the 6- and 12-month windows would have confirmed the trend, costing some of the longer-run compounding.

Neither number is "wrong" — this is the standard responsiveness-vs-persistence trade-off in momentum investing, showing up cleanly in a real backtest instead of just as a theoretical concern. Whether 2.6% points of CAGR is worth 1.1% points of shallower drawdown is a preference call this report doesn't make on your behalf.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Only ONE weighting scheme (50%/30%/20%) was tested — the user-specified weights, not a search over alternatives; a different split (e.g. 40/40/20, or 60/25/15) could land anywhere between the original and this result, or outside that range entirely.
  • All three windows still divide by the SAME trailing-1-year volatility as the risk-adjustment denominator — a genuinely "3-month-native" version might use 3-month volatility for the 3-month leg instead, which was not tested here.
  • Today's fixed NIFTY Midcap 150 constituent list is applied retroactively across the whole window for both formulas equally (survivorship bias) — same disclosed approximation as every other reconstruction here.
  • Zero transaction costs, slippage, or taxes modeled — same as reports 11-19/24-36's frictionless numbers (see report 32 for what real Kotak Neo charges do to a similar strategy).
  • Equal weighting (not free-float market-cap x momentum score) and no F&O-eligibility screen, same as every other reconstruction in this project.
  • Prices are unadjusted; no dividends are modeled for any holding. This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path — a choppier or trendier market regime could shift this specific trade-off in either direction.
