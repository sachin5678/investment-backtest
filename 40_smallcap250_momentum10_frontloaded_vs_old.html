A second broad universe, the same pattern

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Smallcap250's universe (250 stocks) sits between Midcap150 (150) and NIFTY500 (500) in breadth, and its result sits closer to NIFTY500's pattern than Midcap150's: front-loading wins clean here too, and the drawdown improvement is actually the LARGEST of the three universes tested so far. This is consistent with report 39's hypothesis — a broader pool of candidate stocks gives a 3-month-heavy score more genuinely-strong names to fill the top 10 from, diluting the "short-term spike that reverses" risk that a narrower universe is more exposed to.

It's worth noting Smallcap250 stocks are typically less liquid and more volatile than NIFTY500's large/mid-cap-heavy mix, so breadth alone may not be the whole explanation — small caps in general may simply be more prone to the kind of sharp, unwinding drawdowns that a front-loaded score (which reacts faster to deteriorating 3-month momentum too, not just improving momentum) is better at avoiding. Report 41 tests NIFTY100 — a universe narrower than even Midcap150 — to see whether breadth or something else is really driving this pattern.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Only ONE weighting scheme (50/30/20) was tested here — report 38 found the choice between splits itself matters on Midcap150 in a non-obvious way, and that same sensitivity was not re-tested on Smallcap250.
  • All three windows still divide by the SAME trailing-1-year volatility as the risk-adjustment denominator, same simplification disclosed in reports 37-39.
  • No official "Smallcap250 Momentum 10" index exists — the June/December cadence and min-eligible threshold are borrowed conventions from this project's other smallcap reconstructions, not a real index's actual rulebook.
  • Today's fixed Smallcap250 constituent list is applied retroactively across the whole window for both formulas equally (survivorship bias), same disclosed approximation as every other reconstruction here.
  • Zero transaction costs, slippage, or taxes modeled — same as every prior report's frictionless numbers (see report 32 for what real Kotak Neo charges do to a similar strategy). Smallcaps in particular tend to have wider real-world spreads than this backtest assumes.
  • Equal weighting (not free-float market-cap x momentum score) and no F&O-eligibility screen, same as every other reconstruction in this project. Prices are unadjusted; no dividends are modeled. This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path.
