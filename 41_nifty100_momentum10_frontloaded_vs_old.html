Why the narrowest universe hurts front-loading most

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

NIFTY100 is the narrowest and most liquid universe tested in this series — 100 large-cap names, versus 150 (Midcap150), 250 (Smallcap250), and 500 (NIFTY500). With so few candidates, a 3-month-heavy score has almost no "bench" to draw alternatives from: when a large-cap name has a short-term spike (a results beat, a sector re-rating, a one-off news event) that later reverses, it is FAR more likely to occupy one of only 10 slots out of 100 eligible stocks than one of 10 slots out of 500. Large caps also tend to have LOWER idiosyncratic volatility than mid/small caps, so a genuine 3-month move and a temporary spike are harder to tell apart using the same vol-adjustment denominator this formula relies on throughout.

Lining up all four universes by size makes the pattern unusually clean: NIFTY500 (500, win) → Smallcap250 (250, win) → Midcap150 (150, trade-off) → NIFTY100 (100, loss). That ordering by stock count matching the ordering by outcome is the strongest evidence yet for the breadth hypothesis raised in report 39 — though four data points across one set of formula weights (50/30/20) and one historical window is still not enough to call this settled. A useful next test would be varying the front-load weights (like report 38 did for Midcap150) specifically on NIFTY100, to see whether a gentler split narrows or removes this loss.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Only ONE weighting scheme (50/30/20) was tested here — report 38 found the choice between splits itself matters on Midcap150 in a non-obvious way, and that same sensitivity was not re-tested on NIFTY100. A gentler split might behave differently here.
  • All three windows still divide by the SAME trailing-1-year volatility as the risk-adjustment denominator, same simplification disclosed in reports 37-40.
  • Today's fixed NIFTY100 constituent list (report 12's config) is applied retroactively across the whole window for both formulas equally (survivorship bias), same disclosed approximation as every other reconstruction here.
  • Zero transaction costs, slippage, or taxes modeled — same as every prior report's frictionless numbers (see report 32 for what real Kotak Neo charges do to a similar strategy).
  • Equal weighting (not free-float market-cap x momentum score) and no F&O-eligibility screen, same as every other reconstruction in this project. Prices are unadjusted; no dividends are modeled. This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path — a different window could rank these two formulas differently, or narrow the gap seen here.
