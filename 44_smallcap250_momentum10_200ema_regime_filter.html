More volatility, more to protect

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Smallcap stocks are typically the most volatile, least liquid names in the market — which is exactly why Smallcap250 Momentum 10's own unfiltered drawdown (-44.8%) is deeper than Midcap150's or NIFTY500's. A regime filter driven by NIFTY 50's price doesn't know or care which universe it's protecting — it just removes exposure during the SAME broad-market downturns regardless. Applied to a universe with more downside to begin with, the same filter mechanically produces a bigger absolute cut, which is what shows up here.

The same 69-re-entries-vs-36-scheduled-rebalances whipsaw cost from report 42 applies unchanged here — this backtest still charges zero for every switch, and smallcap stocks in particular tend to have wider real-world bid/ask spreads than this project's other universes, so the real transaction cost of that many switches would likely bite harder here than on Midcap150 or NIFTY500.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Zero transaction costs on ANY regime switch — 69 re-entries over 18 years, all free in this model, and smallcaps in particular tend to have wider real-world spreads than this backtest assumes.
  • Cash earns exactly 0% while the filter is out of the market, same convention as reports 03, 42-43.
  • The regime signal and the strategy's own execution both act on the SAME day's close — no next-day execution lag modeled.
  • Only ONE EMA span (200 trading days) is tested here — see report 46 for a span-sensitivity comparison.
  • No official "Smallcap250 Momentum 10" index exists — the June/December cadence and min-eligible threshold are borrowed conventions from this project's other smallcap reconstructions, not a real index's rulebook.
  • Today's fixed Smallcap250 constituent list is applied retroactively across the whole window (survivorship bias). Equal weighting, no F&O-eligibility screen, unadjusted prices, no dividends modeled — same as every other reconstruction here. This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path.
