Why a more relevant signal can still cost return

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

The real NIFTY Smallcap 250 index tends to peak later and trough later than NIFTY 50 during a broad selloff — smallcaps are typically the last to roll over and the last to recover in an Indian market cycle. A signal built on the smallcap index's OWN 200-EMA reacts to that lagged pattern: it exits later than the NIFTY-50 signal would (missing less of the early decline in some cycles, more in others) and — crucially — it also RE-ENTERS later, since smallcaps recover later too. Re-entering later means missing more of the early recovery rally, which is a direct drag on CAGR independent of how good the exit timing was.

This is a genuinely different kind of finding from reports 48-53: it's not a clean win or a clean loss, but a real trade-off between an index that reacts faster (NIFTY 50, more CAGR) and one that's more relevant to what's actually being traded (Smallcap250's own index, less drawdown). Which one an investor should prefer depends on how much they weight return versus downside protection — this report doesn't settle that, it just makes the trade-off explicit.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Zero transaction costs on ANY regime switch — same disclosed omission as reports 42-54.
  • Only ONE EMA span (200 days) and immediate-switch (no confirmation delay) were tested here for either signal source.
  • No official "Smallcap250 Momentum 10" index exists — the June/December cadence is a borrowed convention, though NIFTYSMLCAP250.NS itself IS the real, un-reconstructed NIFTY Smallcap 250 index.
  • Today's fixed Smallcap250 constituent list is applied retroactively (survivorship bias). Equal weighting, no F&O-eligibility screen, unadjusted prices, no dividends modeled — same as every other reconstruction here. This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path.
