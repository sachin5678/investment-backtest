Midcap150 Momentum 10 — Asymmetric EMA Regime Filter

Reports 42-58 used the SAME 200-day EMA for both exiting and re-entering. This report tests exiting on the slow 200-day EMA (unchanged, avoids whipsaw on the way out) but re-entering on a FASTER 50-day EMA — the idea being to catch a market recovery sooner instead of waiting for the slow average to catch up, directly targeting the "always re-enters late" pattern flagged in reports 42, 55, and 56.

● the fix backfires — more than double the whipsaw, worse drawdown

Re-entering faster does NOT catch the recovery cleanly — it mostly catches FALSE starts. CAGR falls from +38.5% (symmetric 200/200) to +37.4% (asymmetric 200/50), and max drawdown gets WORSE, not better: -23.5% to -24.6%.

Re-entries more than DOUBLE, from 69 to 148 — the faster signal re-enters, gets caught in the STILL-slow 200-day exit shortly after, exits again, and repeats, adding exposure to further declines that the original symmetric filter would have avoided entirely.

Why "catch the recovery sooner" backfires

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

A 50-day EMA is much choppier than a 200-day one — it crosses back and forth across the price far more often, which is exactly why it's faster to react in the first place. The problem is that "faster to react" cuts both ways: it re-enters at the first sign of strength, but a first sign of strength during a bear market is very often a temporary bounce, not a genuine turn. The exit condition is STILL the slow 200-day EMA, so once that false start reverses, the position has to fall a meaningful amount further before the slow exit signal triggers again — the asymmetry doesn't add a fast exit to match the fast entry, it just adds MORE round trips into declines that haven't finished yet.

This is a useful lesson about combining signals of different speeds: pairing a fast trigger with a slow one doesn't automatically get "the best of both" — it can just import the fast signal's false-positive rate into a strategy that was otherwise built around the slow signal's reliability.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Only ONE re-entry span (50 days) was tested — a less aggressive choice (e.g. 100 days) might strike a better balance, though report 46 already found narrower spans generally trade CAGR for drawdown rather than improving both.
  • Zero transaction costs on ANY regime switch — with 148 re-entries, this omission matters even more here than in report 42's already-flagged case.
  • Cash earns exactly 0% while out of the market, same convention as every regime-filter report here.
  • Today's fixed Midcap150 constituent list is applied retroactively (survivorship bias). Equal weighting, no F&O-eligibility screen, unadjusted prices, no dividends modeled — same as every other reconstruction here.
