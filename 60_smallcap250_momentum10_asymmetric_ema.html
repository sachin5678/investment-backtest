A second universe, the same false-start problem

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Same explanation as report 59: the 50-day EMA is choppy enough to flag "recovery" on moves that turn out to be temporary bounces within a still-declining market, and the slow 200-day exit signal doesn't cut that exposure short quickly enough to avoid the follow-through decline. Smallcap stocks, being typically MORE volatile than midcaps, would be expected to produce even MORE false starts from a fast signal — consistent with re-entries again more than doubling here.

Two universes now agree: pairing a fast re-entry signal with a slow exit signal doesn't get "the best of both speeds" — it imports the fast signal's false-positive rate into an otherwise slow, reliable filter.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Only ONE re-entry span (50 days) was tested — a less aggressive choice might behave differently.
  • Zero transaction costs on ANY regime switch — with 148 re-entries, this omission matters even more here.
  • Cash earns exactly 0% while out of the market, same convention as every regime-filter report here.
  • No official "Smallcap250 Momentum 10" index exists — the June/December cadence is a borrowed convention. Today's fixed constituent list is applied retroactively (survivorship bias). This is a single, fixed 18-year historical path.
