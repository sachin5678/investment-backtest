▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

Even on the one universe where CAGR nudges up, the drawdown outcome is consistent with reports 59-60: more, faster re-entries mean more exposure to the tail end of declines that the slow 200-day exit hasn't caught up to yet. The small CAGR gain here likely reflects NIFTY100's own lower volatility (large-cap names) producing fewer truly bad false starts than the midcap/smallcap universes — but even the "best case" outcome across three tests still shows the intended benefit (better drawdown) failing to materialize.

Taken together, reports 59-61 are a fairly clean rejection of the "fast re-entry, slow exit" idea across the whole universe-breadth spectrum this project tests — from NIFTY100's 100 large caps down to Smallcap250's 250 smaller, more volatile names.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • Only ONE re-entry span (50 days) was tested — a less aggressive choice might behave differently.
  • Zero transaction costs on ANY regime switch — with 147 re-entries, this omission matters even more here.
  • Cash earns exactly 0% while out of the market, same convention as every regime-filter report here.
  • Today's fixed NIFTY100 constituent list (report 12's config) is applied retroactively (survivorship bias). This is a single, fixed historical path.
