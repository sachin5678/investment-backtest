▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

NIFTY100's own unfiltered drawdown is the shallowest of the three universes tested across reports 68-70 — its worst stretches are milder large-cap-driven declines, closer in character to Midcap150's than to Smallcap250's deeper, sharper ones. Gold's historical tendency to hold up during broad, large-cap-led selloffs lines up well with both Midcap150's and NIFTY100's crash profiles (a clear drawdown improvement on top of the CAGR edge), and roughly a wash on Smallcap250's (report 69) — never a real cost, but not always an extra benefit either.

The practical takeaway across all three reports: gold delivers a clear, large CAGR edge everywhere, and its drawdown effect ranges from a genuine bonus to a neutral wash depending on the universe — checking it against the specific universe being protected (as done here) is worth doing rather than assuming a uniform effect. The liquid-fund assumption, despite being a smaller CAGR edge, is the safer generic default precisely because it never made drawdown worse than cash on any of the three universes tested.

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • The liquid-fund sleeve is a MODELED ASSUMPTION (flat 6% p.a.), not real market data — see report 68's data caveat. Real yields varied roughly 3%-9% across this window.
  • GOLDBEES.NS's real history starts 2009-01-02 — the first two calendar days of this window use a forward/backward-filled gold price rather than a real traded one, a negligible approximation over an 18-year span.
  • This is a SIMPLIFIED implementation: exposure scales the ALREADY-COMPUTED fully-invested strategy's own daily returns, not a re-simulation of actual partial share purchases.
  • Zero transaction costs on continuously adjusting the stock/hedge split.
  • Today's fixed NIFTY100 constituent list (report 12's config) is applied retroactively (survivorship bias). This is a single, fixed historical path.
