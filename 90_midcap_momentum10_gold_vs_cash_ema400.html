● gold beats cash on CAGR without giving up any drawdown protection

Swapping cash for gold during risk-off periods lifts CAGR from +36.3% to +39.8% — a real improvement — while max drawdown is essentially UNCHANGED: -31.5% (cash) vs. -31.6% (gold). Gold doesn't need to fall in lockstep with equities to make this work; it just needs to not be as bad as the crashing stocks the filter is protecting against, which is exactly gold's usual role.

For reference, gold alone over this window returned +13.2% CAGR with a -27.4% drawdown of its own — nowhere near as good as the strategy, but calm enough that holding it during the strategy's own risk-off periods adds return without meaningfully adding risk.

Why gold works here without adding risk

▲ framing

WHAT THIS SHOWS — the mechanism, not just the scoreboard.

The 400-EMA filter's job is to avoid the strategy's own worst stretches, which correlate with broad equity-market selloffs. Gold has historically had LOW or even negative correlation to Indian equities during exactly those stretches — it doesn't need to go UP during a crash to help here, it just needs to not crash alongside equities, which is why swapping it in for cash adds return (+39.8% vs. +36.3%) without meaningfully adding drawdown risk.

This is NOT guaranteed to hold in every future crisis — gold and equities can occasionally fall together during a genuine liquidity crunch (some investors sell everything, including gold, to raise cash) — but across THIS 2010-2026 window, holding gold during the strategy's own risk-off periods was a clean improvement over doing nothing with the cash.

Limitations

▲ read before trusting any number above

WHAT THIS SHOWS — every simplification behind this backtest.

  • GOLDBEES.NS's real history starts 2009-01-02 — the first two calendar days of this window (2008-12-31 to 2009-01-01) use a forward/backward-filled gold price rather than a real traded one, a negligible approximation over an 18-year span.
  • Zero transaction costs on ANY regime switch (into or out of gold) — same disclosed omission as reports 42-47, and switching into/out of an ETF position isn't free in reality.
  • GOLDBEES.NS tracks domestic gold prices in INR, which also move with the rupee's own exchange rate — a INR depreciation can lift GOLDBEES.NS even if USD gold is flat, a real but separate driver from "gold as a crisis hedge."
  • Only ONE EMA span (400 days) and immediate-switch (no confirmation delay) were tested here.
  • Today's fixed Midcap150 constituent list is applied retroactively across the whole window (survivorship bias). Equal weighting, no F&O-eligibility screen, unadjusted prices, no dividends modeled — same as every other reconstruction here.
