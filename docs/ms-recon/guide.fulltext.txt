Step 2 · Conditions The gate: yes or no
A condition is a simple test. Stocks that fail are never ranked, and one you hold that stops passing is sold at the next review. Conditions are optional.

Under the hood The five things a style looks at
Every style is a mix of these plain measures, each over a period from 1 week to 1 year.
SVG Momentum How much the price rose over the period Higher is better
SVG Risk-adjusted The rise divided by how bumpy it was Smooth rises win
SVG Low volatility How calmly the price moves day to day Calmer is better
SVG Steadiness Still rising over both 6 and 12 months Lasting rises win
SVG Liquidity Rupees traded a day, on average (stocks) Easier to trade

Top 20: the buy zone Empty slots are filled from the top of the list.

Optional trend filter When the whole market turns down, step aside into cash (or halve the holdings) until it turns up again.

FAQ Questions people ask
What does a fit of 86% mean?
On the review day, the stock matched your ranking rules better than 86% of the other stocks in your universe. It is a comparison, not a forecast: the best fit ranks first, and your portfolio holds the top ranks.
What is the difference between Conditions and Rank by?
Conditions are a yes/no test: a stock that fails is not ranked at all. Rank by orders the stocks that passed. Think of Conditions as the minimum qualification and Rank by as the interview marks.
What does "6-month momentum" mean exactly?
The percentage change from the close 126 trading days ago to the latest close, with prices adjusted for splits and bonus issues. A week is 5 trading days, a month 21 and a year 252.
Which should I use: Style, Factor Mix or Funnel?
Start with Style: pick a shape and turn the dials. Open Factor Mix to see or change the factors behind it. Use Funnel when you want strict step-by-step rules, such as "only the top half by 1-year momentum".
Why is a stock I like missing from the ranks?
It isn't in the universe you picked, it fails one of your conditions or the price range, or it doesn't have enough history yet for the longest period your rules use.
Should I use short or long periods?
Short periods (1 week to 1 month) follow fast moves but trade more often and pay more costs. Long periods (6 months to 1 year) trade less and are steadier. That is the Speed dial; the Quick check shows the effect in seconds.
Can a falling stock still be ranked?
Yes, if nothing stops it: in a weak market even the best fits can be falling. Add the condition "only rising (6M)", or the trend filter that moves to cash when the index turns down.
Is a good backtest a promise of future returns?
No. A backtest shows how fixed rules would have behaved on past prices, including costs. Markets change. Use it to compare rules and understand drawdowns, not as a forecast.
